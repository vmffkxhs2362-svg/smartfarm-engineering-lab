📚 Thermodynamic Principles of Greenhouse Heat Load Calculations

Estimating peak heating capacity for agricultural greenhouses relies on steady-state conductive and convective heat transfer principles. The peak thermal demand determines the sizing of hot water boilers, heat pumps, and pipe rail distribution loops.

1. Fundamental Heat Loss Equation (Q = A * U * ΔT)

The total heat flux transmitted through the greenhouse envelope is calculated using the classical Fourier conduction equation modified for overall heat transfer coefficients:

  • Q = A * U * (Ti - To) * F_wind (Watts or kW)
  • Where A is total exposed surface area of roof and side walls (m²).
  • U is the thermal transmittance (W/m²·K) of the glazing material.
  • ΔT = Ti - To is the temperature differential between target indoor setpoint and lowest design outdoor temperature.
  • F_wind is the wind speed infiltration multiplier (1.0 at < 1 m/s up to 1.35 at > 7 m/s).

2. Typical Glazing Material U-Values

  • Single PE Film: 6.0 W/m²·K (High transmission, high thermal loss)
  • Double Inflated PE Film: 3.5 W/m²·K (~40% thermal energy savings)
  • Single Float Glass (4mm): 5.5 W/m²·K
  • Twin-Wall Polycarbonate (8mm): 3.3 W/m²·K
  • Triple-Wall Polycarbonate (16mm): 2.3 W/m²·K (Superior insulation)
