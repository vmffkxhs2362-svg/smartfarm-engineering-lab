Greenhouse Heat Loss: A Comprehensive Engineering Guide

Calculating greenhouse heat loss is arguably the most critical step in designing a commercial Controlled Environment Agriculture (CEA) facility in temperate or cold climates. An undersized heating system (boiler or heat pump) leads to catastrophic crop loss during sudden freezes, while an oversized system results in wasted capital expenditure and inefficient short-cycling.

Understanding the Heat Transfer Coefficient (U-Value)

The core variable in heat loss calculations is the U-value (Heat Transfer Coefficient), measured in W/m²·K. The U-value indicates how easily heat escapes through the greenhouse covering material. A lower U-value means better insulation.

  • Single Polyethylene Film (U ≈ 6.0): Cheap and highly transparent, but offers virtually no insulation. Massive heat loss during winter nights.
  • Double Polyethylene with Inflated Air (U ≈ 3.5): The industry standard for cost-effective cold-climate greenhouses. The trapped air layer significantly reduces heat transfer.
  • Standard Single Glass (U ≈ 5.5): Excellent light transmission and longevity, but poor thermal resistance.
  • Twin-wall Polycarbonate (U ≈ 2.3 to 3.3): Excellent insulation and high impact resistance, often used for sidewalls or rigid structural designs.

The Impact of Wind Speed and Convection

Heat loss is not static. While conduction through the material is a baseline, forced convection caused by high outdoor wind speeds aggressively strips heat away from the outer surface of the greenhouse. Our calculator automatically applies a wind-chill friction modifier. A 10 m/s wind can increase your total heating load by up to 30-40% compared to a completely still night.

How to Size Your Heating System (Capacity)

To use this calculator, you must input the total exposed surface area of your greenhouse cover (not just the floor area). Then, select your covering material to set the U-value. Determine your Design Outdoor Temperature (the historical coldest night expected in your region) and your Target Indoor Temperature (usually around 18°C for tomatoes or cucumbers).

The resulting Required Heating Capacity (kW) tells you exactly how powerful your boiler or heat pump array needs to be to maintain your target indoor temperature during the absolute worst-case scenario weather event.
