Optimization of Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD) for Greenhouse Tomato Yield and Quality

VPD Calculator

J. Smith, A. Doe (2023) | Journal of Agricultural Engineering (Open Access)

Vapor pressure deficit (VPD) is a critical climate parameter that directly influences plant transpiration, nutrient uptake, and overall yield in controlled environment agriculture. This study evaluates the effect of maintaining VPD between 0.8 and 1.2 kPa on greenhouse-grown tomatoes. Results indicate a 15% increase in calcium transport to developing fruits and a significant reduction in blossom-end rot (BER) incidence compared to high-humidity (VPD < 0.5 kPa) environments. The findings emphasize the necessity of precise microclimate control utilizing integrated heating and misting systems.

Adapting the Penman-Monteith Equation for Greenhouse Canopy Transpiration Modeling

Transpiration Modeler

L. Garcia, K. Yamamoto (2021) | Frontiers in Plant Science

The Penman-Monteith (P-M) equation remains the standard for estimating evapotranspiration. However, its application in greenhouse environments requires significant adaptation due to zero wind speed and decoupled aerodynamic resistance. This paper presents a modified P-M model tailored for indoor hydroponic canopies, highlighting the dominance of the radiation term over the aerodynamic term. By adjusting the stomatal resistance based on incoming PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation) and canopy Leaf Area Index (LAI), the model achieves a 94% accuracy rate against lysimeter data.

Comparative Analysis of Overall Heat Transfer Coefficients (U-Values) in Modern Greenhouse Glazing Materials

Heat Loss Estimator

H. Müller, C. Davis (2024) | Energy & Buildings in Agriculture

Energy consumption for heating constitutes up to 70% of operational costs in northern latitude greenhouses. This research provides a comprehensive empirical analysis of the overall heat transfer coefficient (U-value) for various glazing materials, including single glass, double-polyethylene film, and multi-wall polycarbonate. We also evaluate the impact of thermal screens on reducing night-time radiative heat loss. The study concludes that deploying a double thermal screen system reduces the effective U-value by 45%, presenting the most cost-effective retrofit for energy conservation.
